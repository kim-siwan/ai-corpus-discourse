Comments in response to the request for information on the development of an AI
action plan

We are writing in response to the request for information on the development of an AI
action plan, issued by the National Science Foundation (NSF) in collaboration with the
Networking and Information Technology Research and Development (NITRD), dated
February 2, 2025.

We are pleased to have the opportunity to share our views on the AI Action Plan to be
developed pursuant to President Trump’s Executive Order 14179 (the “AI EO”). Those
views are set out in this letter in the form of four recommendations.

Background on Enzai

Enzai provides an AI governance, risk and compliance software solution that helps
organizations understand and manage the risks that come with AI, while meeting their
emerging regulatory obligations. Our customers are large, international organizations
(including members of the Fortune 500) around the world that are seeking to make the
most of the opportunity with AI, in a secure and sustainable way. Our team has deep
expertise in developing intuitive and comprehensive AI governance frameworks,
covering all the major laws and standards in this space such as Colorado’s SB 24-205
(known informally as the Colorado AI Act) and the NIST AI Risk Management
Framework.

Recommendations

We believe in AI’s immense potential to improve people’s lives and drive economic
growth. We acknowledge the Trump administration’s emphasis on enabling private
sector innovation. In this section we have set out four recommendations, each of
which has been informed by our close work with private sector organizations as they
seek to deploy AI systems with confidence while mitigating their risks.

It is important to note that these recommendations reflect our view that AI governance
is a key enabler of AI adoption. From our perspective, AI governance is the set of
policies, processes and tools that guide the safe and sustainable development and use
of AI technologies. With a strong AI governance framework in place, an organization
can build trust with its stakeholders while also ensuring it is making the most of the AI
opportunity. This trust removes uncertainty and lowers barriers to AI deployment.

Our four recommendations are set out below.

 Recommendation 1: articulate a positive vision for how AI can help people.

 AI has demonstrated enormous potential in healthcare, business innovation,
 education and many other fields. Given the Trump administration’s focus on “AI
 opportunity”, the AI Action Plan should articulate a positive vision for how AI can
 help people.

 This positive vision should include specific outcomes, such as:

 ● Improving healthcare, while driving down the costs of treatments and
 medications

 By quickly analyzing large, complex data sets, AI has the potential to
 accelerate drug discovery, enable physicians to tailor treatments to each
 person and detect anomalies like tumors and lesions faster and more
 accurately than existing methods. Many leading labs have already
 introduced ‘deep research’ functionality to support such efforts.

 ● Boosting the competitiveness of small businesses

 By analyzing large troves of data at low costs, AI has the potential to
 make certain business insights, that were previously only available to the
 largest organizations, accessible to businesses of all sizes. These
 insights can inform, for example, the identification of new customer
 segments, the development of new product features based on customer
 interactions and the ability to rapidly locate and recruit employees and

 2
 partner organizations.

● Enhancing educational outcomes

 By tailoring educational experiences to each student’s personality, ways
 of learning, resources, life circumstances and interests, AI has the
 potential to enhance educational outcomes at scale. Everything from a
 course’s mode of delivery, its content and its pacing, could be
 personalized to boost meaningful engagement and retention. AI also has
 the potential to develop personalized assessments, to help spot and
 address fundamental knowledge gaps quickly rather than waiting for
 results from scheduled tests.

It is also important to address the concerns about AI’s impact on the global jobs
market, and employment opportunities with the United States. While these
concerns are legitimate, we feel strongly that they will not materialize if the AI
opportunity is managed correctly.

History shows that technological revolutions ultimately create more jobs than
they displace. AI is poised to generate entirely new categories of work that
don't exist today, much as the internet created roles like social media manager,
SEO specialist and app developer. Rather than simply automating existing tasks,
AI will augment human capabilities, enabling workers to focus on higher-value
activities requiring creativity, emotional intelligence and complex
problem-solving.

This transition will create demand for AI trainers, explainability experts, ethics
advisors and human-AI collaboration specialists. By positioning the United
States as a leader in responsible AI innovation, we can ensure these new
economic opportunities benefit American workers and businesses first, creating
a new generation of middle-class jobs while maintaining the United States’
global competitive edge.

Recommendation 2: provide American leadership and funding for
international research efforts related to AI.

Due to their advanced and rapidly improving capabilities, AI systems pose a
unique set of challenges that transcend industries and borders. Given America’s
strengths in AI, American leadership and funding for AI research efforts are
critically important to ensure that AI has the positive impact on the world that
we all want to see.

Therefore, once the administration has defined its positive vision for the societal
benefits of AI, it should support this vision by leading and funding specific
AI-related projects at the frontier of the field, with support from international

 3
collaborators. These projects should focus on both thematic areas (like
explainability research) and sectoral areas (like life sciences), with objectives
that support the administration’s vision.

One outstanding model for such projects is the Human Genome Project
(“HGP”), a bipartisan 13-year effort to identify all the base pairs of the human
genome. Though Americans led the HGP, participants from the United Kingdom,
Japan, France, Germany and China also made significant contributions in
funding and expertise. HGP insights enabled significant private sector
innovation in healthcare and life sciences. Thanks to the HGP, researchers were
able to rapidly sequence the Covid-19 virus and develop vaccines in 2020.

Developing, funding and obtaining international buy-in for specific AI-related
projects will promote American leadership internationally and support private
sector innovation in the long term, while addressing the unique set of
challenges posed by AI.

Recommendation 3: clarify rules for the federal government’s use of AI.

Thanks to OMB Memoranda M-24-10 and M-24-18 (the “OMB AI Guidance”),
federal agencies have compiled and published their AI inventories, appointed
Chief AI Officers and convened AI Governance Boards. These developments
have been a net positive for the AI industry in America, and the Trump
administration should build upon this momentum as it works to revise the OMB
AI Guidance (as envisioned by the AI EO).

Note that even though the considerations, principles, characteristics and
contract terms outlined in the OMB AI Guidance will only apply within
government, these documents send out a clear signal to the private sector
which helps to shape the market through government procurement efforts.

The following steps can help improve the effectiveness of the OMB AI
Guidance:

● Standardize responsible AI principles and characteristics across
 agencies

 While M-24-10 Section 4(e) recognizes the efficiencies to be gained
 from implementing the memorandum’s requirements in a consistent
 manner across federal agencies and M-24-18 Section 3(b) describes
 interagency information and knowledge sharing efforts related to AI
 acquisition, the OMB AI Guidance stops short of standardizing
 responsible AI principles and characteristics across agencies.

 4
 As a result, agencies including the US Department of Commerce, the US
 Food and Drug Administration and the US Department of Defense each
 outline different responsible AI principles and characteristics across their
 various policy instruments. By including common responsible AI
 principles and characteristics in the reworked memoranda, the
 administration can foster inter-agency collaboration, while reducing
 friction for vendors supplying AI systems to the government.

 This streamlined approach would facilitate quicker AI adoption across
 the federal government, which would allow industry to be able to iterate
 on and improve their AI solutions in a much more efficient manner.

● Include sample contract terms for providers of safety-impacting and
 rights-impacting AI systems

 M-24-10 Section 5 provides descriptions and risk frameworks for
 safety-impacting and rights-impacting AI systems. It also provides the
 elements of AI impact assessments for such AI systems, while
 acknowledging that each assessment should be tailored to agency
 needs, AI system characteristics and use case contexts.

 The administration should bolster M-24-10 by supporting its risk
 framework with voluntary, sample contract terms for vendors supplying
 rights- and/or safety-impacting AI systems. By clarifying how,
 specifically, such vendors may be able to demonstrate alignment with
 the memorandum’s requirements, the administration can reduce AI risks
 to rights and safety, provide clarity for vendors and drive efficiency for
 government agencies.

● List agency AI marketplaces that enable faster procurement of AI

 For organizations wishing to sell AI systems to the government, it
 frequently takes longer than a year to become compliant with the
 requirements of the Federal Risk and Authorization Management
 Program (FedRAMP) program. The compliance process also imposes a
 significant financial burden, which can be difficult for smaller
 organizations to meet. This makes it difficult for the government to
 experiment with and procure AI systems from smaller, yet highly
 innovative providers who lack government contracting expertise.

 Some federal agencies have led the way to develop marketplaces that
 rely on faster, alternative procurement vehicles. For example, the US
 Department of Defense developed the Tradewinds marketplace using
 Other Transaction Authority (OTA) to rapidly procure AI systems, while
 the US Department of State announced that it is developing a similar

 5
 marketplace. M-24-18 should both list specific AI marketplaces like
 Tradewinds, to bolster their visibility, and encourage agencies
 developing such marketplaces to extend access to other agencies, to
 promote efficiency and faster procurement.

Recommendation 4: access to data.

Access to high-quality, diverse, and representative data is the foundation of
effective AI systems. While private companies have made significant
investments in proprietary datasets, broader innovation requires addressing
structural challenges to data access that currently limit AI development,
especially for smaller companies and researchers.

The administration should take the following steps to enable responsible data
access while protecting privacy and intellectual property:

● Create public-private data partnerships

 Develop a framework for public-private data partnerships that enables
 companies to securely share non-sensitive data for specific research
 purposes.

 These partnerships could focus on high-impact domains like healthcare,
 climate science and transportation where collaborative data sharing can
 accelerate breakthroughs that benefit society while maintaining
 competitive advantages for participating organizations.

● Establish federal data repositories with clear governance

 Direct federal agencies to create and maintain high-quality, anonymized
 datasets in their domains of expertise, with clear access protocols and
 governance frameworks.

 These repositories should include documentation on data provenance,
 collection methodologies and known limitations to ensure responsible
 use.

 For example, the National Institutes of Health could expand its data
 repositories for biomedical research, while the US Department of
 Transportation could develop standardized datasets for autonomous
 vehicle development.

● Develop privacy-preserving data access techniques

 6
 Fund research and development of advanced privacy-preserving
 technologies such as federated learning, differential privacy and
 synthetic data generation.

 These technologies allow organizations to derive insights from data
 without exposing sensitive information, enabling broader participation in
 the AI ecosystem while protecting individual privacy rights. The
 administration should provide technical standards and best practices for
 implementing these techniques across industries.

 By addressing these data access challenges through thoughtful policy
 interventions, the administration can unlock innovation across the AI ecosystem
 while ensuring that data usage remains responsible and aligned with American
 values of privacy and fairness.
