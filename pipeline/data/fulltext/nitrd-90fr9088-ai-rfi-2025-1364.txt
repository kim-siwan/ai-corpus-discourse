Re: AHAM Comments on NITRD’s Request for Information on the Development of an
 Artificial Intelligence (AI) Action Plan

Dear Mr. D’Souza:

On behalf of the Association of Home Appliance Manufacturers (AHAM), I am writing to
provide our comments to the Office of Science and Technology Policy’s Networking and
Information Technology Research and Development (NITRD) National Coordination Office
(NCO) on its Request for Information on the Development of an Artificial Intelligence (AI)
Action Plan; 90 Fed. Reg. 9088 (Feb. 6, 2025). AHAM supports the development of an AI
Action Plan that defines AI, recognizes the different areas of potential digital activities, and
reviews regulations developed prior to the advent of AI for needed updates. These plan elements
will help the agency develop policy actions needed to support AI innovation and ensure that
unnecessary burdensome requirements do not hamper such innovation.

AHAM considers digital activities and their impacts in four categories:
 • Safety: protection of life, limb, and property;
 • Cybersecurity: protection of digital networks;
 • Privacy: protection of the consumers’ personal data; and
 • Interoperability: creating an Internet of Things where connected devices can seamlessly
 “talk” to one another.

Like many industries, the home appliance industry is still trying to determine how rapid
advances in AI can be deployed in terms of product design and consumer use cases. As part of
their innovative initiatives, our member companies are making those determinations on an
individual basis. Any government AI Action Plan should keep these four activity areas at the top-
of-mind for consumer electrical and electronic devices and AI development should not
compromise progress made in the four categories described above. Any regulations should be
clear, protect consumers, and not burden innovation.

We note that from a policy and government perspective, there is no clear and commonly
accepted definition of AI, particularly as it relates to consumer product applications.
Development of a definition could be a worthwhile result of an AI Action Plan. The term “AI” is
used both with simpler rule-based algorithms and automation and with systems that have
authentic learning and adaptive capabilities. Clear definitions will differentiate between these
two approaches to product design and may, ultimately, lead to more informed consumer choice.
Clarity also supports innovation and decreases the possibility that future regulation will be
burdensome by providing a consistent understanding to innovators early on.

An AI Action Plan should also include a review of government regulations developed before the
advent of AI to determine whether there are potential conflicts between existing government
requirements and AI development. This is critical to ensure regulations do not unnecessarily
hamper innovation.

AHAM appreciates the opportunity to submit comments to NITRD regarding its Request for
Information and would be glad to discuss these matters in more detail should you request.

Respectfully submitted,

Sriram Gopal
Senior Director, Regulatory Policy & Circular Economy
